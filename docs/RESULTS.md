The selected model's MAE is 9.9006 USD/MWh; its RMSE is
18.7753. RMSE weights rare, large misses more strongly. Its mean signed
error is -1.3522 USD/MWh (positive means overprediction).
The training-derived high-price threshold is 146.42
USD/MWh; its MAE on test hours above that threshold is 38.02.
Exact daily peak-hour accuracy is 56.2%.

For the selected model, calibrated intervals cover 60.7% of
test hourly outcomes against an 80% nominal level, with mean width
14.63 USD/MWh. This is empirical coverage, not an operational
reliability promise. The frozen calibration period can differ substantially from 2025.

The paired daily MAE difference versus WeeklyNaive is -15.21
USD/MWh; its seven-day moving-block bootstrap 95% interval is
[-21.27, -9.78]. A negative difference favors
the model. This interval does not measure training-seed variability, protect against
all model comparisons, or establish performance in another year/market.

## Read the comparison carefully

The top row is the best observed test score. The bold row is the model chosen using
validation only. Transformer labels do not imply that those models must win. With only
two training years and a price-history-only information set, simpler approaches can be
competitive. Poor interval coverage and large spike errors are retained in the report.
The experiment supports a comparison for this stated task, not a broad claim of superiority.

Chronos-2 also ran on **363 days** (all eligible 2025 delivery days): MAE **10.32 USD/MWh**, native interval coverage **69.5%**. Its public pretraining overlap is unknown, so it is **reported separately** and excluded from supervised model selection. [Exact record and pinned checkpoint](../reports/foundation_experiment.json).
